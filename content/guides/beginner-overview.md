## What does "competitive" actually mean?

If you've only ever played through the story or battled friends casually, competitive Pokémon Champions might sound intimidating — but it's really just Pokémon battles with a shared, fair set of rules. Every Trainer picks from the same legal pool of Pokémon, battles happen in a structured format instead of anything goes, and the goal is to build a team that works well together rather than just using your favorites.

That's it. You don't need to memorize spreadsheets or watch hours of tournament footage to get started — you just need to understand a few basics.

## How does a match actually work?

A typical competitive match works like this:

- Each Trainer builds a team of Pokémon ahead of time (usually 6, though only some are actually used in a given battle)
- Battles are turn-based: both Trainers choose their moves at the same time, then the game resolves them
- Most competitive formats are **doubles** — two Pokémon per side on the field at once, not one — which means positioning and team synergy matter as much as raw power
- Matches are often played as a **best-of-3** in tournaments, so you may need to adjust your team between games based on what you saw

## What's "the meta," and why does it keep changing?

You'll see the term **regulation** a lot on this site (like "Regulation M-B"). A regulation is simply the current rule set — which Pokémon are allowed, and sometimes other restrictions. When the regulation changes, some Pokémon become legal or illegal, which shifts what's strong. That shift is what people mean by "the meta."

This matters for you as a new player mainly in one way: don't worry about memorizing an old team you saw online — always check what's currently legal and strong before building around it.

## Where to go from here

You don't need to figure any of this out alone — that's what the rest of this site is for:

- **[Tier List](/tier-list)** — a quick answer to "what's actually strong right now," grouped simply from S (best) down, so you're not guessing
- **[Pokédex](/pokedex)** — look up any individual Pokémon to see its stats, common moves, and role, so you understand *why* it's good, not just that it is

A good way to start: browse the Tier List, click into two or three Pokémon that catch your eye, and read their roles. You'll start noticing patterns — some Pokémon support the team, some hit hard, some control the pace of the match. That's really all "team building" is: picking a few of each.
