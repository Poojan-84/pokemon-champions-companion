## What's a "team archetype"?

An archetype is just a recognizable team *shape* — a group of Pokémon that work together toward the same plan, rather than six strong Pokémon picked independently. Once you recognize a few archetypes, building your own team gets a lot easier: instead of asking "which Pokémon are good?" you can ask "which *plan* do I want to run?" and pick pieces that support it.

Here are four archetypes that show up repeatedly in current Regulation M-B tournament play.

## Sun Offense

**Core:** Charizard (Mega Y) + Garchomp + Kingambit

This is the most common structure in the format right now. Mega Charizard Y sets its own sun on entry, which powers up its Fire- and Grass-type attacks and removes the usual charge-up turn from Solar Beam. Garchomp and Kingambit back it up as hard-hitting physical attackers, so the opponent can't just focus all their offense on one threat. The idea: overwhelm with power from multiple angles at once, weather doing quiet extra work in the background.

## Trick Room

**Core:** Sinistcha + Incineroar (or Farigiraf)

Trick Room flips the normal turn order so slower Pokémon move first. Sinistcha is a natural fit — bulky, and it can set Trick Room itself while using Rage Powder to pull attacks onto itself and protect teammates. Incineroar or Farigiraf typically round this out with their own support tools (Fake Out, Helping Hand). This archetype works best when your team is built around a couple of slow, extremely hard-hitting Pokémon that struggle to move first normally — Trick Room is what lets them actually attack before getting hit.

## Rain

**Core:** Pelipper + Archaludon + Swampert (Mega)

Pelipper sets rain on entry, which boosts Water-type attacks and gives Hurricane perfect accuracy. Archaludon and Mega Swampert take advantage of the weather with strong Water/Electric coverage. Like Sun Offense, this is a weather-support archetype — one Pokémon's entire job is setting the stage, and the rest of the team is built to cash in on it.

## Sand Offense

**Core:** Tyranitar (Mega) + Excadrill

[Tyranitar](/pokedex/tyranitar) sets sandstorm, which boosts its own Special Defense and synergizes with [Excadrill](/pokedex/excadrill)'s Sand Rush ability — Excadrill's Speed doubles in sand, turning it from a slow attacker into one of the fastest Pokémon in the format for as long as the weather holds. This archetype rewards timing: knowing when to bring Excadrill in *after* sand is already up.

## The pattern across all four

<div class="callout">
<p><strong>Notice something: three of these four archetypes are built around weather</strong> (sun, rain, sand). That's a real, current feature of the Regulation M-B meta, not a coincidence.</p>
</div>

Weather-setting Pokémon are strong right now, so teams built around exploiting weather are common. If you're building your first team, picking one weather archetype and building around it is a genuinely solid, low-complexity starting point.
